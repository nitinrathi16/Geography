Factors Affecting India's Climate

6 Major Controlling Factors

📌 UPPSC Key Concept: India's climate is essentially a Tropical Monsoon Climate (Koppen: Aw/Cw types), but shows enormous regional diversity due to these controlling factors.
🌍 1. Latitude
📍India spans 8°4'N to 37°6'N — tropical to sub-tropical
☀️Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) divides India into tropical south and sub-tropical north
🌡️Southern India (Kerala, TN) = high temp year-round; Northern India = extremes
🎯UPPSC Fact States south of Tropic of Cancer: Kerala, TN, AP, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra (part), MP (part), Rajasthan (part)
⛰️ 2. Himalayas (Most Critical)
🛡️Barrier function: Prevents cold Siberian winds from entering India — keeps India warmer than similar latitudes globally
🌧️Orographic rainfall: Forces SW monsoon winds to rise, causing heavy rainfall on windward side (Western Himalayas, NE India)
❄️Leeward side (Tibet, Central Asia) = rain shadow = arid
🌊Traps monsoon moisture in Indian subcontinent
🌊 3. Indian Ocean & Seas
🔵India surrounded by Arabian Sea (W), Bay of Bengal (E), Indian Ocean (S)
💧Provides moisture for SW Monsoon — source of precipitation
🌡️Moderating effect: Coastal areas have moderate temp (Mumbai vs Delhi — same latitude)
🌀Bay of Bengal: warmer, more cyclone-prone; Arabian Sea: fewer cyclones
📏 4. Distance from Sea (Continentality)
📌Interior locations (Delhi, Nagpur, Jodhpur) = continental climate — extreme temperatures
🏖️Coastal locations (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi) = maritime climate — moderate temperatures
🌡️Delhi: max temp 45°C, min -1°C; Mumbai: 38°C to 12°C (much narrower range)
💧Interior areas get less rainfall from sea winds
🏔️ 5. Altitude / Relief
❄️Temperature decreases 6.5°C per 1000m rise in altitude
🌨️Shimla (2200m) = snow in winter; Mussorie, Darjeeling = hill stations
🌧️Orographic rain: Western Ghats windward = Kerala, coastal Karnataka gets 300+ cm rainfall; leeward (Deccan) = rain shadow
📊Cherrapunji & Mawsynram (Meghalaya) = highest rainfall in world due to orographic effect
💨 6. Upper Air Circulation (Jet Streams)
🌬️Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream: Flows south of Himalayas in winter; bifurcates in May-June triggering monsoon
🔄Tibetan Anticyclone: Develops over Tibet plateau in summer; drives upper-level easterlies over India
⚡ITCZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone): Moves north to ~25°N in summer, triggering SW monsoon onset
🎯UPPSC 2021 Q Western disturbances cause winter rainfall in NW India — brought by mid-latitude westerlies

Southwest Monsoon — Complete Analysis

Onset, Mechanism, Retreat — UPPSC Level Deep Dive

🌧️ Key Fact: SW Monsoon brings 75-90% of India's annual rainfall from June to September. It's the lifeline of Indian agriculture and economy.
⚙️ Mechanism of SW Monsoon — Step by Step
1
☀️ Differential Heating (April-May)
Asian landmass heats up rapidly. Low pressure develops over NW India (Thar Desert), Pakistan, and Northwest India. Ocean heats slowly — high pressure remains over Indian Ocean. This creates pressure gradient from sea to land.
2
🌊 ITCZ Migration (May-June)
Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) shifts northward from Equator to ~25°N over the Indian subcontinent. Trade winds from Southern Hemisphere cross Equator, deflected rightward by Coriolis effect — become SW winds.
3
💨 Jet Stream Bifurcation (May)
Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream, which flows south of Himalayas in winter, splits into two arms: one north of Himalayas and one south. This triggers monsoon onset. Tibetan Plateau heats up creating anticyclone aloft — drives Easterly Jet Stream over India.
4
🌬️ Monsoon Onset — Two Branches
Arabian Sea Branch: Hits Kerala coast first → splits into 3 sub-branches (Konkan, Gujarat-Maharashtra; Western Ghats-Central India; Goa-Karnataka). Bay of Bengal Branch: Moves along Arakan coast → hits NE India first → moves westward along Ganga plains.
5
⛰️ Orographic & Convergence Rainfall
Both branches converge over Indo-Gangetic plains. Orographic barriers (Western Ghats, NE hills) force air upward → cooling → condensation → rainfall. Tamil Nadu coast lies in rain shadow of Arabian Sea branch and parallel to BoB branch — gets less SW monsoon rain.

Northeast (Retreating) Monsoon

Tamil Nadu's lifeline — October to December

🔑 Core Concept: NE Monsoon blows from land to sea (NE → SW), picks up moisture from Bay of Bengal, and gives Tamil Nadu its primary rainfall season (Oct-Dec).
⚙️ Mechanism
1
Land cools rapidly (Oct-Nov)
After SW monsoon retreat, continental India cools; ocean retains heat → High pressure over land, Low pressure over sea
2
Winds reverse direction
Cold, dry winds blow from NE India and Central Asia → move towards low pressure over Indian Ocean. Direction: NE → SW (hence "Northeast Monsoon")
3
Moisture pickup over Bay of Bengal
Winds pass over warm Bay of Bengal → pick up moisture → hit Tamil Nadu's Coromandel Coast as moist, rain-bearing winds
4
Rainfall on TN, SE AP, Puducherry
Eastern coast of Tamil Nadu, SE Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, and southern Andaman Islands receive rainfall from October to December
📊 Key Facts — NE Monsoon
🗓️Duration: October to December (peak Nov-Dec)
📍States affected: Tamil Nadu (primary), SE Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, South Karnataka coast, Andaman & Nicobar
🌧️TN contribution: 60% of Tamil Nadu's total annual rainfall comes from NE monsoon (vs 25% from SW)
🌡️Dry nature initially: Winds start as dry, cold continental winds; become moist only after crossing Bay of Bengal
🌊Cyclones: Bay of Bengal most active during NE monsoon (Oct-Dec) — Chennai Cyclone 2015, Cyclone Nilam, etc.
❓Why TN gets less SW monsoon? Tamil Nadu coast is parallel to BoB branch (no orographic barrier) + in rain shadow of Arabian Sea branch (Western Ghats block)

Tropical Cyclones — Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea

Formation, frequency & impact

🌀 Definition: Tropical cyclones are intense low-pressure systems with sustained winds >63 km/h (Cyclonic Storm) or >89 km/h (Severe Cyclonic Storm). They form over warm tropical oceans (SST > 26-27°C).

🌊 Bay of Bengal

📊Frequency: ~80% of all Indian cyclones from BoB
🌡️Warmer SST, shallower sea → ideal cyclone formation
🗓️Peak seasons: Oct-Nov (post-monsoon) & May-June (pre-monsoon)
📍States affected: Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, Bangladesh
💥More destructive — warm deep ocean maintains intensity until landfall
🌀Major: Cyclone Fani (2019, Odisha), Amphan (2020, Bengal), Yaas (2021), Biparjoy (2023)

☀️ Arabian Sea

📊Frequency: ~20% of Indian cyclones from Arabian Sea
🌡️Cooler upwelling waters in S Arabia → less favorable; BUT warming trend now!
🗓️Peak: May-June (pre-monsoon); occasional post-monsoon
📍States affected: Gujarat (most), Maharashtra, Goa, Kerala
🌊Weakens faster — drier air intrusion from Arabian Peninsula
🌀Major: Cyclone Tauktae (2021, Gujarat), Biparjoy (2023), Vayu (2019)

🌀 Cyclone Naming: India follows WMO naming convention. Regional Specialized Meteorological Centre (RSMC) for North Indian Ocean is IMD, New Delhi. The naming panel includes: Bangladesh, India, Iran, Maldives, Myanmar, Oman, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Thailand, UAE, Yemen.
⚠️ Odisha — India's Most Cyclone-Prone State
Odisha has been hit by major cyclones in 1999, 2013 (Phailin), 2014 (Hudhud), 2019 (Fani). Reasons: shallow continental shelf + funnel-shaped BoB + low coastal elevation.
📍Most vulnerable districts: Kendrapara, Bhadrak, Jagatsinghpur, Balasore, Puri
🏛️IMD cyclone warning system: 3-day advance warning; 4-stage alert (Yellow → Orange → Red)
🌿Mangrove forests (Bhitarkanika) act as natural cyclone barriers
🏗️India has Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF) — model disaster response

Climate Change & Indian Monsoon

Impacts, trends & policy response

📌 Key Fact: India's average temperature has risen by ~0.6°C since 1901. IMD data shows increasing frequency of extreme weather events (droughts, floods, cyclones) since 1970s.

🌊 El Niño & Indian Monsoon
🌡️El Niño: Warming of central/eastern Pacific → Walker Circulation weakens → Indian Ocean becomes relatively cooler → Weakens Indian monsoon
🌧️El Niño years with weak monsoon: 1987, 1997, 2002, 2009, 2014-15, 2023
💧La Niña: Cooling of Pacific → stronger Walker Circulation → Enhances Indian monsoon → flood years
🌊IOD (Indian Ocean Dipole): Positive IOD (W Indian Ocean warmer) → more rainfall; Negative IOD → deficit rainfall
🏛️ India's Policy Response
📋NAPCC 2008: National Action Plan on Climate Change — 8 missions including National Water Mission, Solar Mission
🌿Green India Mission: Increase forest cover by 5 million ha; carbon sink
☀️Paris Agreement (2015): India's NDC — 45% reduction in emission intensity by 2030; 50% renewable energy
🌱ISA (International Solar Alliance): India-France initiative; HQ Gurugram
🌊CDRI: Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure — India led, 2019
🎯 UPPSC 2018: India's 1st National Action Plan on Climate Change = 2008
🏔️ Climate Change in Himalayas & Himalayan Rivers
IPCC warns: Hindu Kush Himalayan (HKH) region warming 2x faster than global average. By 2100, 2/3 of Himalayan glaciers could disappear under high-emission scenario.
❄️Gangotri Glacier retreating ~22m/year
💧Short term: More glacial melt → more river flow; Long term: rivers reduce (Ganga at risk)
⚠️GLOF (Glacial Lake Outburst Floods): Uttarakhand 2021 Chamoli disaster
🌡️Snowline rising → Himalayan snow pack reducing → affects spring flow (rabi irrigation in UP)
🌧️Rainfall patterns shifting from winter snowfall to summer rain → increased flood risk
🌿Tree line shifting upward; Himalayan biodiversity under stress

1 Which of the following correctly describes the mechanism of Southwest Monsoon onset over India?
a Cooling of Tibetan Plateau causes monsoon winds
b Differential heating of land and sea + ITCZ migration northward + bifurcation of Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream
c High pressure over Arabian Sea drives winds towards land
d Southeast trade winds from Southern Hemisphere directly enter India
✅ Answer: (b) SW Monsoon is caused by: (1) Low pressure over NW India due to intense heating of Thar Desert, (2) ITCZ migration to ~25°N, (3) SE Trade winds cross Equator → deflected right by Coriolis → SW winds, (4) Bifurcation of Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream in May-June triggers rapid monsoon onset. The Tibetan Plateau HEATS up in summer (not cools), creating anticyclone aloft and Easterly Jet Stream.
2 Arrange the following in the correct order of Southwest Monsoon onset (earliest to latest):
1. Delhi   2. Kerala   3. NE India (Assam)   4. Rajasthan (NW)   5. Andaman & Nicobar
a 5 → 2 → 3 → 1 → 4
b 2 → 5 → 3 → 1 → 4
c 3 → 2 → 5 → 1 → 4
d 5 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 4
✅ Answer: (a) Andaman & Nicobar Islands (May 15) → Kerala (June 1) → NE India/Assam (June 1-5) → Delhi (June 29) → NW Rajasthan (July 10-15). A&N Islands get monsoon earliest in India!
3 Consider the following statements about Northeast Monsoon:

1. It blows from NE to SW direction.
2. It is the primary source of rainfall for Tamil Nadu (about 60%).
3. It is moisture-laden from the start as it originates over the Arabian Sea.
4. It is most active during October to December.

Which of the above statements are CORRECT?
a 1, 2 and 4 only
b 2, 3 and 4 only
c 1, 2 and 4 only
d All four are correct
✅ Answer: (c) → Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Statement 3 is WRONG: NE monsoon originates as dry, cold continental winds from NE India/Central Asia. It picks up moisture only AFTER crossing the Bay of Bengal, not at origin. Statements 1 (direction NE→SW), 2 (60% TN rainfall), and 4 (Oct-Dec) are all correct.
4 Which of the following is the CORRECT reason why Cherrapunji/Mawsynram receives the highest rainfall in the world?
a It is located at the highest elevation in NE India
b It lies at the junction of both Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches
c It has the maximum number of lakes and wetlands
d It lies on the southern edge of Meghalaya Plateau facing Bay of Bengal, creating funnel-shaped orographic uplift
✅ Answer: (d) Cherrapunji and Mawsynram are located on the southern slopes of the Meghalaya Plateau. The plateau acts like a funnel — BoB branch monsoon winds rush into this funnel-shaped valley and are forced upward rapidly. This intense orographic lifting causes condensation and extraordinary rainfall (1141-1492 cm/year). It's not about elevation alone — it's about the funnel geometry of the terrain.
5 'Loo' is a hot, dry wind which blows in summer. Which of the following correctly describes it?
a It blows from East to West; affects only Rajasthan
b It blows from West/NW to East; affects Punjab to Bihar including UP; blows in May-June afternoons
c It is a cold wind that brings relief from summer heat
d It is a local wind only in Rajasthan and Gujarat
✅ Answer: (b) Loo is a hot, dry, desiccating wind that blows from West/NW direction across the Indo-Gangetic plains, covering Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Rajasthan. Temperature during Loo: 45-48°C; very low relative humidity; blows in afternoons; peak May-June. It is responsible for deadly heat wave deaths in UP and Bihar. UPPSC 2024 specifically asked about Loo and its geographic extent "Punjab to Bihar."
6 Which of the following statements regarding cyclones is INCORRECT?
a Bay of Bengal accounts for about 80% of Indian cyclones
b Cyclones in Bay of Bengal are more frequent during October-December
c Arabian Sea cyclones are more frequent and intense than Bay of Bengal cyclones
d Sea Surface Temperature above 26-27°C is necessary for cyclone formation
✅ Answer: (c) This statement is INCORRECT. Bay of Bengal cyclones are both MORE FREQUENT (~80% of Indian cyclones) and historically MORE INTENSE than Arabian Sea cyclones. Arabian Sea has fewer cyclones due to drier air intrusion from Arabian Peninsula and cooler upwelling waters. However, in recent years (due to climate change) Arabian Sea cyclone frequency is INCREASING (Tauktae 2021, Biparjoy 2023) — but BoB still dominates.

8 Consider the following about rainfall distribution in India:

1. Western Rajasthan receives less than 25 cm of annual rainfall.
2. Tamil Nadu coast gets maximum rainfall during Southwest Monsoon season.
3. Mawsynram (Meghalaya) receives average annual rainfall of about 1000 cm+.
4. Leeward side of Western Ghats (Deccan Plateau) receives more than 100 cm of rainfall.

How many of the above statements are CORRECT?
a Only one
b Only two (1 and 3)
c Only three
d All four
✅ Answer: (b) Only two — Statements 1 and 3 are correct.
Statement 1 ✅: W. Rajasthan gets <25 cm (Jaisalmer ~15 cm).
Statement 2 ❌: Tamil Nadu coast gets MINIMUM rainfall during SW Monsoon — it's in rain shadow. It gets maximum rain in NE Monsoon (Oct-Dec).
Statement 3 ✅: Mawsynram receives ~1492 cm/year — highest in world.
Statement 4 ❌: Leeward/eastern Deccan side gets only 40-70 cm — it's in the rain shadow zone.

10 With reference to Uttar Pradesh's climate and its Bundelkhand region, consider the following statements:

1. Bundelkhand region (Jhansi, Banda, Chitrakoot) is categorized as drought-prone due to erratic monsoon and rocky terrain.
2. The districts of Gorakhpur, Lakhimpur, and Bahraich in UP are prone to floods from the Ghaghra/Saryu river system.
3. SW Monsoon retreats from Western UP by October 15, later than Eastern UP.
4. UP receives all its rainfall from the Bay of Bengal branch of SW Monsoon alone.

Which of the above statements are CORRECT?
a 1 and 2 only
b 2 and 3 only
c 1, 2 and 4 only
d All four
✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only.
Statement 1 ✅: Bundelkhand is drought-prone — confirmed fact.
Statement 2 ✅: Gorakhpur, Lakhimpur, Bahraich are flood-prone from Ghaghra (Saryu) and its tributaries.
Statement 3 ❌: Monsoon retreats from WESTERN UP FIRST (~Sept 15-20), not last. Eastern UP's monsoon retreats later (~Sept 25). The retreat moves from NW to SE.
